The regulatory backbone — the piece that keeps Art-is-Tree's name off a violation notice.

The permitting engine

What it does

  • CBPA / RPA screening. Geocodes the property and tests it against each city's GIS map layers (RPA / parcel) to flag whether it sits in a Chesapeake Bay–protected buffer. Cities covered: Virginia Beach, Norfolk, Chesapeake, Portsmouth.
  • Right-form retrieval + in-app fill. Pulls the correct city's current application and pre-fills it from the property record.
  • Map + tree-labeling tool. A parcel/aerial map where you drop a pin per tree and tag species, size, and remove / retain / replace, with the RPA line drawn on it — exported as a city-style site plan (the format VB and others expect).
  • Packet assembly + Gmail permit-history mining. Recognizes city contacts and links prior cases to properties.
Hard guardrails (see file 03): it never says “you're clear” — always “verify with the city.” GIS is unreliable at parcel edges, and a false “clear” is exactly what produced the Circle Drive violation. It prepares and hands off; a human submits through the city portal or email.

Virginia tree law (verified in-session)

  • Ownership follows the trunk. A tree belongs to whoever's land the trunk sits on, no matter where the branches hang — this never changed, and it's the point you raised and were right about.
  • Self-help on encroachment: Fancher v. Fagella (2007) is still current, good law on what a neighbor may do about branches/roots crossing the line.

Messaging & consent

  • TCPA compliance for any automated texting — consent, STOP handling, quiet hours — engineered in from the start. Flagged as a real requirement, not a nice-to-have; worth an attorney's review.
  • Neighbor e-consent “permission slip.” A link the neighboring homeowner clicks to “allow” access/overhang work, creating a legal permission record.

Safety & equipment standards

  • ANSI Z133 — the arboriculture safety standard; source material for the crew clock-in safety quiz.
  • VA VOSH (VOCEA) training packet — the state occupational-safety basis for crew training gates.
  • OSHA / ANSI load-plan requirement — the crane estimator is built to support the requirement that a load plan be established before a lift (estimate only).
