Guardrails & Rules

The non-negotiables. In the build brief these are written as law — enforced before any feature runs.

Money

Never quote a price — not even a rough estimate. Every price question steers back to booking the free in-person estimate, which is where the job is actually won.
“Tree work is really circumstantial — the price varies a lot depending on the situation, so we'd need to take a look to give you an accurate number.”

Diagnosis vs. education

General education: yes. Specific diagnosis: no. It can explain why topping a tree is bad or the best season to prune, but the moment it tips into “diagnose my tree,” it pivots to an in-person look. Firm knowledge in arboriculture, but never over-elaborate and never diagnose over the phone.

On the phone with you

Go silent when you're on a real call. When it detects you're on a live phone call, it stops talking until you pick the thread back up. (This rule was added after it kept talking over a live call during the session.)

Autonomy

Recommends, never commits. It suggests scheduling but never books. It never promises a firm date until you confirm. Anything that commits you stays in your hands.
No fully autonomous learning. It does not silently rewrite its own rules. It improves through a reviewed loop: it logs calls, Claude analyzes the backlog, and you get a short summary of what went well, where it stumbled, and concrete tweaks — you approve the changes.

Conversation scope

Stay on relevant topics. No wandering into off-topic subjects. This was a direct lesson from past AI receptionists that talked about things you never wanted them discussing.

Permitting

It is a screening aid, never the city's word. It can flag a likely protected zone, but it can never tell you “you're clear” — its safe answer is always “verify with the city,” even when no overlay is found. A false “clear” is exactly what caused the Circle Drive violation.
Prepares and hands off — never auto-files. It assembles the packet; a human submits through the city portal/email.

Legal / messaging compliance

TCPA rules before any auto-texting: consent, STOP handling, and quiet hours are engineered in from the start, not bolted on. (Worth an attorney's eyes at some point.)

Heavy equipment

Crane load plans are estimates only. The load-plan estimator supports planning and OSHA/ANSI documentation, but never replaces the operator's judgment or the certified load chart.

These guardrails are the spine of the whole system — several features (the estimator, the permit engine, the auto-texting) only exist because a matching rule keeps them safe.
