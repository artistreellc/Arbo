## A. Permits and public works

### A.1 Virginia Beach

**1. Tree on private property**
- **Rule.** No general permit was found for removing a tree on a single-family lot outside protected areas. A permit is required inside the Chesapeake Bay Preservation Area (CBPA) Resource Protection Area (RPA), in the public right-of-way, and in tree-preservation areas shown on approved site plans. This comes from a third-party summary and was not confirmed on a city page (https://cityrulelookup.com/cities/virginia-beach-va/landscaping).
- **What the RPA covers.** Tidal wetlands, tidal shores, connected non-tidal wetlands, plus a **100-ft vegetated buffer** landward of them and along perennial streams. Mature trees are to be pruned instead of removed where possible, with removals kept to the fewest feasible (state regulation 9VAC25-830: https://register.dls.virginia.gov/details.aspx?id=10307).
- **Form.** The city uses a "Preliminary Project Request – Tree Removal Only" form for RPA projects, listed under Land Development (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/land-development). Its fee and required contents are **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **Who reviews.** Planning Dept, Environmental Planning (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/environment). Exceptions go to the Chesapeake Bay Preservation Area Board (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/boards-commissions/chesapeake-bay-board).
- **Phones.** Environmental planner lines 757-385-6661, 757-385-8615 and 757-385-8594. The Development Services Center main line is 757-385-4621 (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/permits/right-of-way). Planning main was also given as 757-385-4211.
- **Dead or diseased trees in the RPA.** No VB-specific rule was found. The general state and locality pattern is that removal needs the administrator's approval, often with a certified-arborist letter, and replacement vegetation. **UNCONFIRMED for VB.**
- **Hazard orders (City Code § 23-50.1).** The code enforcement administrator or city arborist can order a dead, diseased or dangerous tree removed within 7–30 days. If the owner doesn't act, the city charges the cost plus a $150 admin fee (http://virginiabeach.elaws.us/code/coor_ch23_arti_sec23-50.1).
- **Wetlands.** The VB Wetlands Board reviews use or alteration of tidal wetlands, dunes and beaches (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/boards-commissions/wetlands-board).

**2. Street and right-of-way trees**
- **Owner.** Parks & Recreation, Park & Landscape Services. It maintains roadways and assesses and removes hazardous trees on public property (https://parks.virginiabeach.gov/outdoors/park-landscape-services, https://parks.virginiabeach.gov/outdoors/caring-for-our-parks/urban-forestry).
- **Private work on city trees.** A city code excerpt says the city manager or designee may permit cutting or trimming of street trees "only when such work is fully justified in the public interest," done as the permit directs, under the city arborist or superintendent of landscape services. The source is a Virginia Tech 2016 abstract (http://vtod.frec.vt.edu/Documents/Virginia%20Beach.pdf), so the current section number is **UNCONFIRMED**.

**3. Working in the street**
- **Permit.** A right-of-way permit is required for "any activity within a City-owned right of way or easement." The Development Services Center issues it: Municipal Center Bldg 3, 2403 Courthouse Dr, 757-385-4621 (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/permits/right-of-way).
- **Seasonal ban.** No sidewalk or lane closures at the Oceanfront or Sandbridge from May 1 to Sep 30 unless the Public Works Director grants a waiver (same page).
- Fees and lead time: **UNCONFIRMED**.

**4. Historic rules.** The Historic Preservation Commission is advisory, created in 2008 (https://planning.virginiabeach.gov/boards-commissions/historic-preservation-commission). No historic-district tree review was found. **UNCONFIRMED.**

**5. Yard waste disposal**
- The city Landfill & Resource Recovery Center (1989 Jake Sears Rd) accepts **no commercial yard waste**. Vehicles with commercial signage or work equipment are turned away (https://pw.virginiabeach.gov/trash-recycling/landfill-and-rrc).
- City yard-debris containers may not be used by contractors, and contractor-generated debris must be hauled by the contractor (https://pw.virginiabeach.gov/trash-recycling/curbside-services/yard-debris).
- Private option: Hampton Roads Recovery Center, 1613 Centerville Tpk, 757-420-0128 (https://www.wasteconnections.com/hampton-roads-recovery-center). Whether it accepts green waste is **UNCONFIRMED**.

### A.2 Norfolk

**1. Tree on private property**
- **Rule.** It is unlawful to "plant, transplant, trim, prune, remove or otherwise disturb any tree, shrub or other vegetation" in the RPA or Intensely Developed Area (IDA) of the CBPA Overlay District without a permit. "All trees/shrubs within the 100' CBPA buffer are protected… A permit is required for removal." This applies to **pruning too**, which is stricter than the other cities (https://www.norfolk.gov/DocumentCenter/View/65912/Chesapeake-Bay-Preservation-Area-Tree-and-Shrub-information, https://www.norfolk.gov/Faq.aspx?QID=666).
- **Mitigation.** Required by size class, species and location. Trees of 36 in DBH or more are "large specimen" trees, and their mitigation depends on a risk assessment by an ISA Certified Arborist (https://norfolk.gov/DocumentCenter/View/23107/Norfolk-tree-mitigation-standards-2015).
- **Who reviews.** Environmental Services, CBPA (https://www.norfolk.gov/2752/Environmental-Services).
- **Phone conflict.** Snippets gave **757-664-4368** (Environmental Services, from the CBPA document) and 757-664-4752 (Planning). Which is current is **UNCONFIRMED**.
- Outside the CBPA, no general private-tree permit was found. **UNCONFIRMED.**

**2. Street trees**
- **Rule.** No one except authorized Parks & Forestry staff may plant, prune, trim or remove trees on city property without a permit. A Tree Permit Application is needed even for work "in close proximity to any city tree" (https://norfolk.gov/DocumentCenter/View/917/Tree-Street-Application).
- **Contacts.** treepermits@norfolk.gov. Parks & Urban Forestry, 2839 Dana St, 757-823-4023; that phone may be out of date (https://www.norfolk.gov/5457/Urban-Forestry).
- **Exemption.** Residents may prune crape-myrtle base suckers under 1 in in the strip next to their own property without a permit.

**3. Working in the street**
- **Office.** Right-of-Way Management Division, permits office 757-664-7306 (https://norfolk.gov/345/Right-of-Way-Management).
- **Permits.** Temporary street, lane and sidewalk closure permits. Work follows the ROW Excavation & Restoration Manual and the Virginia Work Area Protection Manual.
- **Lead time.** Submit the application, plans and a **traffic control plan (maintenance of traffic, "MOT") 7–14 days ahead**.
- **Fee.** **$25 non-refundable application fee on closure and ROW permits from July 1, 2026** (https://www.norfolk.gov/4543/Permits-Fees).

**4. Historic districts.** The Architectural Review Board issues Certificates of Appropriateness (COAs) for exterior changes in local historic districts. A "General COA" covers minor alterations "including landscaping" (https://www.norfolk.gov/1090/Architectural-Review-Board, https://norfolk.gov/DocumentCenter/View/21150/Historic-Districts-Brochure-COA). Whether removing a tree on private property in a district needs a COA is **UNCONFIRMED**; the snippet was an inference. Right-of-way trees go to the City Forester, not the ARB.

**5. Yard waste.** "Lawn services and contractors are responsible for removing yard waste generated" (https://norfolk.gov/402/Yard-Waste-Recycling).

### A.3 Chesapeake

**1. Tree on private property**
- **Rule.** Vegetation removal is limited to what the CBPA ordinance allows. The RPA must keep a **100-ft woody buffer with at least 50% canopy**. Removal is allowed only under an approved buffer-modification plan: sight lines, access paths, woodlot management, best management practices (BMPs) and shoreline projects.
- **Dead trees.** Dead trees, or trees weakened by age, storm or fire, may be excluded at the discretion of the Director of Development & Permits.
- **Exception route.** Other prohibited RPA work needs a CBPA Exception application, with a Board hearing about 40–45 days after filing.
- **Ordinance update.** The CBPA ordinance was amended in 2025, and a new Specifications Manual is dated 11/05/2025.
- **Sources.** https://library.municode.com/va/chesapeake/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH26EN_ARTIXCHBAPRARDI, https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/DocumentCenter/View/29520/CBPA-Specifications-Manual-2025-PDF, https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/DocumentCenter/View/16222/CBPA-Exception-Application-Instructions-PDF, https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/548/Chesapeake-Bay-Preservation-Area-CBPA.
- **Phone.** Development & Permits, 757-382-6018.
- **Site plans.** Tree preservation on site plans falls under the Landscape & Tree Preservation Ordinance (https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/400/Landscape-Tree-Preservation).

**2. Right-of-way trees**
- **Owner.** The Urban Forestry Division, within Parks, Recreation & Tourism (https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/3693/Urban-Forestry).
- **City Tree Removal Policy.** The city removes dead, leaning or diseased city trees, but not private trees and not city-tree limbs overhanging private property. Removal by the adjacent owner "at their expense with proper City right-of-way permits" is case by case and needs Public Works Director approval (https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/1007/City-of-Chesapeake-Tree-Removal-Policy).
- **Requests.** 757-382-CITY (2489).

**3. Working in the street.** The Civil Permits Section issues ROW and miscellaneous ROW permits. Bond and inspection fees are scoped to the plan (https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/448/Civil-Permits, https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/1008/Permits-Bonds-Right-of-Way). Info line 757-382-8298. Fee and lead time are **UNCONFIRMED**.

**4. Historic district.** The Board of Historic & Architectural Review covers significant exterior changes visible from the street in the South Norfolk district (https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/1244/Chesapeake-Board-of-Historic-Architectur). Tree coverage is **UNCONFIRMED**.

**5. Yard waste.** No city contractor drop-off was found. **UNCONFIRMED.**

### A.5 Regional rules

**Regional yard waste (SPSA).**
- SPSA transfer stations take yard waste. Loose material must be bagged, and limbs must be **under 6 ft long and 6 in in diameter**. Box trucks and cargo vans pay the commercial tipping fee (https://www.spsava.gov/162/Residential-Waste-Disposal-Guidelines, https://www.spsava.gov/35/Acceptable-Materials, https://www.spsava.gov/193/Regional-Landfill).
- Regional Landfill (Suffolk) phone 757-961-3683.
- The per-ton rate and whether green waste is taken at the landfill face are **UNCONFIRMED**.

**Wetlands.** Tidal-wetland work goes through the Joint Permit Application to the Virginia Marine Resources Commission (VMRC), with local wetlands boards, DEQ and the Army Corps reviewing (https://www.vims.edu/ccrm/advisory/ccrmp/handbook/permits/, https://www.mrc.virginia.gov/regulations/hm-permits.shtm).

**VA811 (Miss Utility)**
- **Notice.** No excavation without notifying VA811 first. The minimum is **2 working days**; operators have 48 hours from 7:00 a.m. the next working day to mark. A ticket is valid **15 working days**, and re-marks are requested 3 working days before it expires (Va. Code § 56-265.17: https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title56/chapter10.3/section56-265.17/).
- **Stumps.** VA811 lists **"removing a stump"** as digging (https://va811.com/excavators/damage-prevention-act-2/). "Stump grinding or removal" is a ticket work type (https://va811.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/work-type-list.pdf). Treat stump grinding as needing a ticket.

**Power lines**
- **Dominion Energy.** Only qualified utility line-clearance contractors may work within **10 ft** of power lines, or on a tree with branches within 10 ft. Call **1-866-DOM-HELP (366-4357)**. Dominion does not trim around service drops but will temporarily disconnect the drop so a contractor can work (https://www.dominionenergy.com/safety/electric-safety/tree-trimming-and-powerlines/va-tree-trimming-and-powerlines).
- **State law.** The Virginia Overhead High Voltage Line Safety Act (Va. Code § 59.1-406 et seq.) bars work within **10 ft of lines at 600 V or more** without safety arrangements made with the utility. Notice goes to the utility under § 59.1-411, and the utility must start arrangements within **5 working days**. Violators indemnify the utility (https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title59.1/chapter30/, https://doli.virginia.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Program-Directive-12246A.pdf).
- **OSHA and ANSI Z133 approach distances.**
  - Qualified line-clearance arborists work under OSHA 1910.269 minimum approach distances.
  - Everyone else keeps **10 ft for lines up to 50 kV**, more at higher voltages (https://tcimag.tcia.org/safety/part-5-work-practices-near-utility-conductors-different-categories-of-tree-workers/, https://tcimag.tcia.org/safety/incidental-line-clearance-what-arborists-need-to-know/).
  - The ANSI Z133-2026 revision restructures Section 4. "Incidental line clearance" becomes "Level 2 Arborist – Electrically Qualified" (https://tcimag.tcia.org/training/ansi-z133-2026-the-progression-of-electrical-qualifications/). TCIA calls it "pending," so its final publication status is **UNCONFIRMED**.

**Contractor licensing (Virginia DPOR)**
- **Classes, effective July 1, 2025:**
  - Class C: more than $1,000 and under $30,000 per project.
  - Class B: $30,000 to under $150,000.
  - Class A: $150,000 and up.
- Sources: https://www.dpor.virginia.gov/sites/default/files/boards/Contractors/Contractor_Consumer_Info.pdf, https://www.dpor.virginia.gov/News/AgencyLegislation2025.
- **Tree work.** The claim that pure trimming needs no license, while stump grinding and land disturbance do, comes only from third-party sites, not from DPOR (https://contractorlicenserequirements.com/virginia/landscaping-license-requirements/). **UNCONFIRMED.**

**Burning**
- **State 4 PM law, Feb 15–Apr 30.** No open burning before 4 p.m. within 300 ft of woods or dry grass; burning is allowed 4 p.m. to midnight. The fine is up to $500 plus suppression costs (https://www.dof.virginia.gov/wildland-prescribed-fire/fire-laws/4-pm-burning-law/).
- **Virginia Beach.** Leaves, limbs and yard debris are **not approved** for open burning (https://fire.virginiabeach.gov/fire-prevention/fire-regulations/open-burning-recreational-fires).
- **Chesapeake.** Land-clearing and commercial burning need an operational permit from the Fire Marshal (https://www.cityofchesapeake.net/3402/Regulations-for-Open-Burning).
- Norfolk and Portsmouth rules: **UNCONFIRMED**.

## B. LiDAR from a phone in a web app

**1. Can a web page read the LiDAR?** No, on iPhone.
- Safari on iOS and iPadOS does not implement WebXR. Handheld WebXR AR is "still not exposed by Safari in 2026" (https://xrdoctors.pro/blog/webxr-on-ios-what-actually-works, https://launch.variant3d.com/blog/23-06-state-webxr-on-ios-beyond).
- visionOS Safari supports immersive-vr only (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/756850).
- One blog claims Safari 18 added inline AR on iPhone (https://multiwaresolutions.com/blog/spatial-computing-webxr-in-safari-18-2026-06-08). That conflicts with the other sources and is **UNCONFIRMED; likely wrong**.
- LiDAR depth is exposed only through native AVFoundation and ARKit (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/AVFoundation/capturing-depth-using-the-lidar-camera). No getUserMedia depth stream was found.

On Android, the **WebXR Depth API is shipped in Chrome**, using ARCore on supported devices (https://chromestatus.com/feature/5742647199137792, https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/webxr/arcore-comparison, spec https://www.w3.org/TR/webxr-depth-sensing-1/).

**What this means for the app.** Scan in a native app, export the file, then have the web app take it in through `<input type="file">`. The Web Share Target API (being a "Share to" destination) **does not work on iOS**; it works on Chrome Android 76+ (https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/share_target).

**2. iOS scanner apps and their exports**

- **Scale.** ARKit and LiDAR captures are metric. Polycam states depth in meters and lets you choose metric or imperial (https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/38276871185044-How-to-Extract-Raw-Data-and-What-Is-Included). That every app's PLY is in meters is **UNCONFIRMED**; verify with a sample file.
- **Range.** Effective range is about 5 m, with clean data around 3–4 m (https://www.simplywise.com/blog/iphone-lidar-construction-accuracy/, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-01763-9).

**3. Measuring trees from a phone point cloud**
- **DBH (iPad Pro, Gollob et al. 2021).** RMSE of 3.13 cm with SiteScape, 3.64 cm with 3d Scanner App and 4.51 cm with Polycam, against 1.5–1.9 cm for a €50k handheld laser scanner. Tree detection was 76.7–98.1% (https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13163129).
- **DBH (ForestScanner).** RMSE about 2.3 cm, R² 0.96 (https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.13900).
- **DBH (research single-shot iPhone 13 Pro method).** RMSE 0.63 cm (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12390307/).
- **Bias.** ForestScanner and Arboreal Forest both tend to underestimate DBH (https://academic.oup.com/forestscience/article/70/4/304/7664433).
- **Height and crown.** The roughly 5 m range makes phone LiDAR poor for mature-tree height. Studies report underestimation even for seedlings (https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2688-8319.12399, https://doi.org/10.3390/s25185629). No validated phone-LiDAR crown-spread method was found. **UNCONFIRMED.**
- **Desktop tools.**
  - **3D Forest**: open source; extracts stem position, height, DBH and crown metrics (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0176871).
  - **3DFin**: CloudCompare/QGIS plugin; DBH RMSE under 2 cm on terrestrial laser scans (https://github.com/3DFin/3DFin).
  - **TreeQSM**: single-tree structure models (https://github.com/InverseTampere/TreeQSM).
  - These are validated mainly on terrestrial and mobile laser data, not phone data.

**4. Arborist apps that export data**
- **ForestScanner.** Free; CSV of stem positions and DBH.
- **Arboreal Forest.** CSV (Trees, Stems, Heights) and KML (https://arboreal.se/en/blog/share-download-data-from-arboreal-forest).
- **Arboreal Tree.** Measures height, DBH, crown width and height, and lean. LiDAR support, GPS tag, CSV export (https://arboreal.se/en/tree-height).

## C. Installable web app on iOS and Android, 2026

**iOS and iPadOS**
- **iOS 26: every site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app by default.** The user can switch off "Open as Web App." A manifest `display` value is no longer needed (https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/, https://webkit.org/blog/16993/news-from-wwdc25-web-technology-coming-this-fall-in-safari-26-beta/).
- **Icons.**
  - Manifest icons have been supported since Safari 15.4, used when there is no apple-touch-icon and `purpose` is "any" or omitted.
  - An `apple-touch-icon` takes precedence (https://webkit.org/blog/12445/new-webkit-features-in-safari-15-4/).
  - Common practice is one opaque 180×180 PNG (https://favicontools.com/glossary/apple-touch-icon); this is secondary-source.
- **Status bar.** `apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style` accepts default, black and black-translucent. One source says black-translucent is deprecated. **UNCONFIRMED.**
- **Splash screens.** Safari ignores the manifest `background_color` for the splash. It needs exact-size `apple-touch-startup-image` links per device and orientation, or it shows black until first paint (https://favicontools.com/glossary/apple-touch-startup-image). This is secondary-source.
- **Web Push (iOS 16.4+).** Only for Home Screen web apps. Permission must be requested from a user gesture. It uses a service worker plus the Push and Notifications APIs, and the Badging API is supported (https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/).
- **Storage.** Safari deletes script-writable storage after 7 days of no use. That covers IndexedDB, localStorage and service-worker registrations. Home Screen apps keep their own day count based on actual use. Persistent-mode storage or an active page exempts an origin (https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria).
- **Missing on iOS.** No Background Sync or Periodic Background Sync, and no automatic install prompt (https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide). Queue data and sync when the app is next in the foreground.

**Android Chrome**
- **Manifest.** Needs HTTPS and a linked manifest with `name` or `short_name`, **192×192 and 512×512 icons**, a `start_url`, and `display` set to standalone, fullscreen or minimal-ui (https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/pwa/installable-manifest).
- **Service worker.** A fetch-handler service worker is **no longer required** for menu installation since Chrome 108 on mobile and 112 on desktop (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/update-install-criteria). Whether the automatic install prompt still wants one is unclear from sources. **UNCONFIRMED.**
- **Maskable icon.** Add an icon with `"purpose": "maskable"` and keep content inside the central 80% safe zone (https://web.dev/articles/maskable-icon).
